01 / THE PROBLEM

Onboarding students.
Managing a paper trail.

Masai needed to collect income-share agreements, codes of conduct, and other student documents. The paperwork had become an operational bottleneck.

Manual data entry introduced errors. Couriers added dependencies to sending, correcting, signing, and returning documents. Each student’s documents became a physical bundle that someone had to store and find again.

The customer described the end-to-end process as taking four to six weeks. The design problem extended beyond a signature: the entire chain of preparation, coordination, and retrieval needed attention.

01

Repeated work

Individual document preparation and data entry increased effort and opportunities for error.

02

Fragile handoffs

Document changes, delivery issues, and student availability disrupted progress.

03

Scattered records

Physical storage made document search and retrieval slow and labor-intensive.

03 / PRODUCT JUDGMENT

Fast first.
Sustainable next.

The first solution needed to unblock the customer. The next needed to make the workflow less expensive to operate.

06 / REFLECTION

The workflow is the product.

The value came from connecting the steps—not treating document creation, signing, and storage as separate problems.

The sequencing lesson is equally important: an integration can solve the immediate need while a native experience addresses the longer-term cost. In evaluating a workflow like this today, I would measure correction rates, completion by cohort, and time spent chasing exceptions alongside turnaround time.
